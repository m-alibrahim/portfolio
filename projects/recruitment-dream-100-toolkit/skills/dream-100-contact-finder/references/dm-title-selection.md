# Decision-Maker Title Selection

How to pick which 3 decision-makers to target at each company, based on company size and the agency's niche.

The goal is to find the 3 people most likely to (a) have hiring authority, (b) feel pain about the open roles, and (c) be responsive to recruitment-agency outreach.

---

## Rule 1: Company size drives the title selection

### <50 employees (Startup)

At this size, the **founder/CEO usually owns hiring** directly. There's rarely a dedicated talent function.

**Target titles (priority order):**
1. CEO / Founder / Co-founder / Managing Director
2. CTO (if the agency niche is tech)
3. CFO (if the agency niche is finance)
4. Head of People / People Lead (if they have one)
5. COO

**Avoid:** Recruiters/TA Coordinators — they may not exist yet, and even if they do, hiring decisions still flow through the founder.

### 50-200 employees (Scale-up)

The talent function emerges in this band, but the founder still has heavy involvement in senior hires.

**Target titles (priority order):**
1. Head of Talent / Talent Acquisition Lead
2. HR Director / People Operations Lead / CPO
3. The relevant functional VP for the agency's niche (see "Functional VP mapping" below)
4. CEO / Founder (especially for senior hires)
5. Head of [function] — e.g. Head of Engineering for a tech recruiter

### 200-1000 employees (Mid-market)

Mature talent function. Senior recruitment still often goes through agencies.

**Target titles (priority order):**
1. The functional VP for the niche (VP Engineering for tech recruiters, etc.)
2. Head of Talent Acquisition / TA Director
3. Senior Talent Acquisition Partner / Manager (for specific function — e.g. "Engineering TA Partner")
4. CPO / VP People
5. Specific function's hiring manager (Director of Engineering, etc.)

### 1000+ employees (Enterprise)

Functional VPs and TA partners own hiring for their slice. The CHRO is too senior to be the right outreach target.

**Target titles (priority order):**
1. Functional VP for the niche (VP Engineering, VP Finance, etc.)
2. TA Director or Senior TA Partner for that specific function
3. Director-level hiring manager within the function (e.g. Director of Backend Engineering)
4. Talent Acquisition Lead — function-specific
5. Head of Recruiting (function-specific if available)

**Avoid:** CHRO, Chief People Officer, generic HR Director — too senior or too generalist.

---

## Rule 2: Functional VP mapping per niche

Match the agency's niche to the functional VP title:

| Agency niche | Target functional VP |
|---|---|
| Software engineering / tech | VP Engineering, CTO, Head of Engineering |
| Data / ML / AI | VP Data, Head of Data Science, Head of ML |
| Product | VP Product, CPO, Head of Product |
| Design / UX | VP Design, Head of Design |
| Sales | VP Sales, CRO, Sales Director |
| Marketing | VP Marketing, CMO, Head of Marketing |
| Finance | VP Finance, CFO, Head of Finance, Finance Director |
| Legal | General Counsel, Head of Legal |
| Operations | COO, VP Operations, Head of Ops |
| HR / People | CPO, VP People (rare — usually a peer hire, not an agency target) |
| Customer success | VP Customer Success, Head of CS |
| Healthcare clinical | Chief Medical Officer, Head of Clinical |
| Construction / trades | Operations Director, Site Manager, Project Director |
| Skilled trades (electrical, etc.) | Operations Manager, Service Manager |

If the niche isn't in this table, infer the closest equivalent — and capture as the functional VP title.

---

## Rule 3: Diversify the 3 DMs at each company

Don't pick 3 of the same title at one company. Diversification gives the agency multiple shots.

**Ideal 3-DM mix per company (50-1000 size band):**
- DM 1 (priority 1): Functional VP for the niche (e.g. VP Engineering)
- DM 2 (priority 2): Head of Talent / TA Lead
- DM 3 (priority 3): Founder/CEO (if findable) OR a Director-level hiring manager

**For startups (<50):**
- DM 1: Founder/CEO
- DM 2: CTO / functional lead
- DM 3: Head of People if exists, else another co-founder or department head

**For enterprise (1000+):**
- DM 1: Functional VP
- DM 2: TA Director for that function
- DM 3: Director-level hiring manager within the function

---

## Rule 4: Flag fresh starters

If the Exa snippet says "joined X company N months ago" (and N < 12), note it in the Contacts notes column. **New hires are great Dream 100 targets** — they have a fresh mandate, often a hiring budget, and they remember agencies who helped them at their last role.

Example notes:
- "Joined as Head of Talent 4 months ago"
- "New VP Engineering — started Sept 2025"

These get a slight priority bump for outreach.

---

## Rule 5: Avoid these titles

These rarely convert for recruitment agency outreach — don't add them to the 3 even if they show up in search:

- Recruiter / Talent Sourcer / TA Coordinator (too junior, no budget authority)
- Intern / Associate (junior)
- Office Manager / EA (not hiring decision makers)
- Chief of Staff (handles VIP work, not agency relationships)
- Generic "Manager" without a function attached
- CEO at companies >2000 employees (too senior to engage directly)
- CHRO at any size (too senior/strategic for tactical agency outreach)

If a search keeps surfacing only these titles for a company, the company may genuinely not have the right DMs — flag it in the notes and consider this in the dmPriority scoring.
