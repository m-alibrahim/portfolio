# Touchpoint Templates

Proven format, length, and tone for each of the 11 Dream 100 outreach assets. Use these as scaffolds — fill with hook + personalization.

---

## Voice & tone — proven copy patterns

These are the patterns that convert in recruitment Dream 100 outreach. Every written asset should mirror them.

**Mandatory patterns:**
1. **Open with curiosity, not pitch.** "Hey [First Name], curious if this is relevant for you or someone else at [Company]?" — beats "Hi I'm X from Y agency."
2. **Mirror their company name back.** Phrase positioning as "companies like [Company]" not "companies".
3. **Compress the positioning to one line.** "We help companies like [Company] hire for [Niche] roles across [Location]."
4. **Recent placement social proof, named.** "We've just placed [Role titles] with [Named clients]." Named clients > anonymous.
5. **Specific job trigger.** Reference a specific role they have open — "I've seen a [Job Title] advertised at [Company]."
6. **Candidate as the hook (when available).** "We've just met a candidate who's [one-line candidate USP]" — the candidate IS the value drop.
7. **Soft media CTA.** "Is it worth me sharing a quick video to introduce?" / "I'll share a quick voice note to intro." Never ask for a meeting in the first message.
8. **P.S. risk reversal.** "p.s. we only charge upon successful hire" — drops the perceived risk.

**Voice rules:**
- Short sentences. One thought each.
- No corporate language: avoid "synergies", "circle back", "touch base", "leverage", "value-add", "stakeholders".
- British-spelt unless agency is US-based.
- Casual but not sloppy. "Hey" beats "Dear". Contractions are fine.
- No hedging openers ("Sorry to bother you", "I know you're busy").
- One CTA per asset, always specific and small.

**Anti-patterns — never do:**
- "I came across your profile"
- "I love what you're doing at [Company]"
- "Hope this email finds you well"
- "Just bumping this to the top of your inbox"
- Generic flattery of any kind

## 1. Lumpy Letter

**Channel:** Physical mail
**Length:** 150-200 words
**Tone:** Warm, professional, personal — like writing to a peer
**Envelope:** Handwritten, marked "Private and Confidential" or "Do Not Bend"
**Gift:** Include a relevant book (Shoe Dog, Built to Sell, The Ultimate Sales Machine, etc.)

**Structure:**
1. Opening — name them by first name, reference something specific you know about them or the company (the hook)
2. Brief context — who you are, one line on what you do
3. The gift — explain the book and why you sent it ("I thought you'd enjoy it" / "Reminded me of what you're building")
4. The ask — "I'll ring you on [day] at around [time] to say hi properly" (specific day/time — sets up the warm follow-up call)
5. Sign-off — first name + signature, mobile number

**Example skeleton:**
```
Hi [First Name],

[Specific company fact — the hook]. Couldn't help reaching out.

I run [Agency Name] — we [one-line specialism — e.g. "place senior backend engineers at Series B fintechs in London"].

I've enclosed a copy of [Book Title] — it [one-line why]. Hopefully a useful read between meetings.

I'll give you a quick ring on Thursday around 10am — nothing to pitch, just want to introduce myself properly.

Best,
[Sender first name]
[Mobile number]
```

## 2. Follow-up Call Script

**Channel:** Phone, 2-3 minutes
**Length:** ~100 words of talking points
**Tone:** Casual, low-pressure
**Timing:** 2-3 days after the letter arrives

**Talking points:**
1. "Hi [First Name], it's [Sender] from [Agency] — did you get the letter and the book?"
2. Light banter on the book / their reaction
3. Why you reached out — the hook in one sentence ("Saw you raised Series B and are hiring 5 engineers")
4. Soft value drop — one specific thing the agency can do for them ("We placed 3 senior Rust engineers at [Similar Company] last quarter")
5. Ask — NOT a sales meeting. Ask for something smaller: "Would you mind if I sent over two profiles to take a look at?" / "Could I send you our 2026 salary guide for senior engineers in [city]?"
6. Close — "Either way, great to connect. I'll follow up by email."

**If voicemail:** Leave a 20-second version. "Hi [First Name], it's [Sender] from [Agency] — sent you a letter and a book a few days ago, just wanted to follow up. I'll drop you a quick email — speak soon."

## 3. Loom Email

**Channel:** Email
**Length:** Subject + 80-120 word body
**Tone:** Curious, helpful, candidate-led
**Purpose:** Get the click on the Loom thumbnail. The video does the selling.

**Subject lines (pick the one that fits):**
- "Quick question"
- "{{firstName}} — quick one"
- "Worth a look? — [Specific role] candidate"
- "Candidate for your [Role] role"

**Body template (mirrors proven recruitment voice):**

```
Hey [First Name], curious if this is relevant for you or someone else at [Company]?

We help companies like [Company] hire for [Niche] roles across [Location]. We've just placed [Specific role titles] with [2-3 named clients].

Specifically I'm reaching out as I've seen a [leadTitle] job advertised at [Company] and we've just met a candidate who's [leadCandidate — one-line USP].

Is it worth me sharing a quick video to introduce?

Best,
[Sender first name]

p.s. we only charge upon successful hire
```

**When [leadCandidate] is not available** (no specific candidate to spec in), substitute with a value asset offer:

```
Hey [First Name], curious if this is relevant for you or someone else at [Company]?

We help companies like [Company] hire for [Niche] roles across [Location]. We've just placed [Specific role titles] with [2-3 named clients].

Specifically I'm reaching out as I've seen a [leadTitle] job advertised at [Company] — I put together a quick salary benchmark for [Niche] roles in [Location] you might find useful.

Mind if I send it over?

Best,
[Sender first name]

p.s. we only charge upon successful hire
```

## 4. Loom Video Script (90 sec)

**Channel:** Loom video (face-cam + screen share if relevant)
**Length:** 90 seconds max
**Tone:** Warm, conversational, smile when you start

**Structure (rough seconds):**
- **0-10 sec — Hook + name:** "Hi [First Name], I'm [Sender] from [Agency]. Made this quick video for you because [specific company fact from the hook]."
- **10-30 sec — The relevance:** Why this matters to them. Show the data / the job listing / the news article on screen if relevant.
- **30-60 sec — The value:** What you've done for similar companies. One concrete proof point. Numbers if possible.
- **60-80 sec — The ask:** Specific, small. "Mind if I send you two candidate profiles to take a look at?" / "Open to a 10-min chat next week?"
- **80-90 sec — Sign-off:** "Either way, hope this is useful. Speak soon."

**Tips:**
- Look at camera, not your reflection
- Lead with their name on the thumbnail screen for maximum click-through
- Keep energy up — boredom kills the click

## 5. LinkedIn Connection Note

**Channel:** LinkedIn connection request
**Length:** Under 300 characters (LinkedIn's hard cap)
**Tone:** Polite, specific, no pitch

**Structure:**
- Name them
- One specific reason for connecting (the hook, compressed)
- No CTA — connection itself is the only ask

**Example skeleton:**
```
Hi [First Name] — saw [specific fact, e.g. "your team's Series B announcement"]. I work with senior [role type] in [niche/geo] and follow what [Company] is building. Would love to connect.
```

Count characters before submitting. LinkedIn rejects over-limit notes.

## 6. LinkedIn Opening Message (post-connect)

**Channel:** LinkedIn DM, sent within 24 hours of accepting connection
**Length:** ~60-75 words
**Tone:** Compressed version of the Loom email voice — same hooks, fewer words

**Template (mirrors proven recruitment voice — voice-note CTA):**

```
Great to connect [First Name] — we help companies like [Company] hire [Niche]. We just helped [2-3 named clients]. Saw your [leadTitle] advertised, we've just met a candidate who's [leadCandidate — one-line USP]. I'll share a quick voice note to intro.
```

**When no specific candidate** (substitute with curiosity opener + value asset):

```
Great to connect [First Name] — we help companies like [Company] hire [Niche]. We just helped [2-3 named clients]. Saw your [leadTitle] advertised — put together a quick salary benchmark for [Niche] roles in [Location], think you'd find it useful. Mind if I share?
```

**Variation — pure curiosity opener** (for very low-info prospects):

```
Hey [First Name] — curious if this one's relevant? We help companies like [Company] hire for [Niche] roles across [Location], and we've just placed [Role titles] with [Named clients]. Worth a quick chat to see if we could help with [leadTitle] or similar?
```

**Send the voice note within 5 minutes of this message.** The text message sets up the voice note — don't make them wait.

## 7. LinkedIn Selfie Video Script (60 sec)

**Channel:** LinkedIn DM, vertical selfie video
**Length:** 60 seconds max
**Tone:** Energetic, personal — looks like you recorded it just for them

**Structure:**
- **0-5 sec:** "Hi [First Name] — recorded this quickly for you."
- **5-20 sec:** The hook — name the specific thing you saw
- **20-45 sec:** What you can help with — one concrete proof point
- **45-60 sec:** The ask — small, specific. "DM me back if you want me to send over [thing]."

**Tips:**
- Vertical orientation (9:16)
- Good light, plain background
- Don't read a script word-for-word — bullet points only

## 8. Voice Note Script (60-90 sec)

**Channel:** Voice note DM (LinkedIn, Instagram, WhatsApp if you have their number)
**Length:** 60-90 seconds
**Tone:** Conversational, like leaving a friend a voicemail — but tighter

**Structure (same as the selfie video, audio only):**
1. "Hi [First Name], it's [Sender] from [Agency]"
2. The hook (specific)
3. One sentence on what you can help with
4. One small, specific ask
5. "No rush — just thought it'd be quicker to record this than type it out"

**Tips:**
- Smile while recording — it changes the voice
- Pause once for breath, makes it feel less scripted
- Don't ramble — if you go over 90 sec, re-record

## 9. Value Brochure Concept

**Channel:** PDF emailed / DM'd / posted physically
**Length:** Concept brief — the agency designs the actual PDF in Canva
**Tone:** Educational, data-led, no sales pitch inside the brochure itself

**Output for this asset:** Topic + 5-7 section outline.

**Strong topics for recruitment Dream 100:**
- Salary guide for [niche role] in [geography] [year]
- [Niche] hiring trends report — Q[X] [year]
- Compensation benchmark: [seniority level] [niche role]
- Why top [niche] candidates leave — exit interview data from [N] placements
- The hiring funnel benchmark: time-to-hire / offer-acceptance / source mix for [niche]

**Outline skeleton:**
```
Title: [Specific to this prospect's situation]

1. Executive summary (one page)
2. [Data section 1 — e.g. salary ranges by seniority]
3. [Data section 2 — e.g. geographic breakdown]
4. [Trend / insight — e.g. "Remote vs in-office offer-acceptance rates"]
5. [Benchmark — how their company compares]
6. [Practical takeaway]
7. About [Agency Name]
```

**Customization:** The outline should reference the prospect's specific situation — e.g. if they raised Series B and are hiring 5 engineers, the brochure could include a section titled "What top engineers expect from Series B companies in 2026".

## 10. Candidate Spec-In Message

**Channel:** Email or LinkedIn DM
**Length:** ~80 words
**Tone:** Direct, helpful, low-stakes
**When to use:** When the agency has a real candidate who'd fit the prospect's open role (or even when they don't have an open role but the candidate is exceptional)

**Structure:**
1. Name + greeting
2. One-line context — why this candidate, why this company
3. Anonymous candidate snapshot — title, years experience, current company tier (e.g. "Senior Engineer, 8 yrs, ex-FAANG"), key skill match
4. The ask — "Would you take 5 mins to look at her profile? No fee involved unless you actually hire."

**Skeleton:**
```
Hi [First Name],

We've been working with a [seniority] [role] who's actively interviewing — [one-line standout fact, e.g. "scaled engineering teams from 5 to 30 at two Series B fintechs"].

She's a strong match for [specific open role at company / specific function]. Worth 5 mins to look at her profile?

No commitment — just thought you should see her before [competitor] does.
```

## 11. Social Engagement Starter Pack

**Channel:** LinkedIn comments on the DM's posts
**Length:** 3 sample comments, 20-50 words each
**Tone:** Genuine, insightful — no flattery, no emojis (unless the DM uses them)

**Goal:** Become visible in their feed. After 3-5 thoughtful comments over a few weeks, the DM recognizes your name when the next outreach lands.

**Comment types to produce:**
1. **Add insight** — share a related fact, stat, or anecdote that extends their point
2. **Constructive challenge** — politely add a "yes, and / but consider X" — be respectful
3. **Ask a sharp question** — one that demonstrates you've actually read and thought about it

**Skeleton:**
```
Comment 1 (insight): "[Sharp observation that builds on their post — e.g. specific data point or counter-example]"

Comment 2 (challenge): "[Polite but smart pushback or alternative angle]"

Comment 3 (question): "[A sharp question that shows engagement]"
```

**Rule:** All 3 comments must reference SOMETHING specific from a real recent post. Generic "Great post!" comments are worse than silence.

## Universal rules across all assets

- **Use first name.** Never "Dear Sir/Madam" or "Hi there".
- **One specific fact per asset.** No "I love your work."
- **One CTA per asset.** Don't pile asks.
- **Match tone to channel.** A lumpy letter can be slightly more formal; a voice note should sound spontaneous.
- **Pass the "annoying" test.** If you'd be irritated to receive this, rewrite it.
